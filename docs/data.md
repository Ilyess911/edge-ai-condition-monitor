Two tracks, never mixed in one number.

| | Simulated rotating machine | MetroPT-3 |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Synthetic**, generated by `src/data/simulator.py` | **Real**, metro train air compressor (Porto, 2020) |
| Why it is here | kHz vibration streams with exact fault labels: needed to test paced streaming, sampling rate and detection delay | Evidence that the pipeline does something on data nobody designed |
| Channels | vibration, temperature, current, speed, pressure | 7 analog (pressures, oil temperature, motor current), 8 digital |

## 1. Simulated rotating machine

**This data is synthetic.** Every constant is a modelling choice. It is designed
to be plausible and hard enough to separate the detectors, not to reproduce a
specific machine.

### Physics, deliberately simple

- **Operating regime.** Speed switches between 1200, 1500 and 1800 rpm and load
  between 0.35 and 0.9, every 60 to 240 s, with 5 s linear ramps. Normal operation
  is therefore non-stationary. The ramps turned out to be the dominant source of
  false alarms (see JOURNAL.md).
- **Vibration.** 1x shaft component scaling with speed², a 2x component, and
  Gaussian noise scaling with load. Generated at 4 kHz and decimated to the ADC
  rate with `scipy.signal.resample_poly` (polyphase anti-aliasing filter), as a
  real acquisition chain would.
- **Current** grows with load x speed. **Temperature** follows a first-order
  thermal model (`tau = 120 s`) driven by current. **Pressure** follows the pump
  affinity law (`p ~ speed²`).

### Injected faults (random severity 0.3 to 1.0)

| Fault | Signature | Duration | Onset |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bearing_outer_race` | impacts at BPFO = 3.58x shaft speed, each ringing a 350 Hz resonance; +2 °C | 120-300 s | progressive (10 % to 100 % of severity) |
| `imbalance` | 1x amplitude x (1 + 2·severity) | 60-180 s | step |
| `cooling_failure` | equilibrium temperature +18 °C x severity | 180-400 s | progressive |
| `cavitation` | pressure −15 % x severity, broadband vibration x (1 + 1.5·severity), current −8 % | 60-180 s | step |
| `electrical_overload` | current +35 % x severity | 20-60 s | step |

The bearing resonance is **scaled down**: real bearing resonances sit at several
kHz, which is why industrial vibration monitoring samples at 10 to 25 kHz. At
350 Hz the simulation stays cheap and the sampling-rate experiment still shows
what is lost when the ADC is too slow.

`electrical_overload` is **not observable** from these channels by design of the
physics: load is not measured, so +16 % current at medium load looks exactly like
normal operation at higher load. No detector should be expected to find it
reliably, and none does.

### Sensor artefacts (labelled NORMAL)

One 200 ms dropout (NaN) per minute and one single-sample x25 glitch per 30 s,
on the slow channels. They exercise the plausibility check and sample-and-hold
of `src/preprocessing/cleaning.py`.

### Splits and seeds

| Run | Duration | Seed | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| train | 60 min healthy | 1 | fit detectors |
| calibration | 30 min healthy | 2 | threshold (q = 0.995) and alert persistence |
| development | 60 min, 5 faults | 101 | building and debugging the pipeline; **never reported** |
| test | 5 x 60 min, 5 faults each | 201-205 | every reported number |

Fault schedules come from an RNG stream independent of the signal noise, with at
least 240 s of healthy operation between faults.

## 2. MetroPT-3

- Source: [UCI Machine Learning Repository, dataset 791](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/791/metropt+3+dataset),
  licence **CC BY 4.0**.
- Paper: B. Veloso, R. P. Ribeiro, P. M. Pereira, J. Gama. *The MetroPT dataset
  for predictive maintenance.* Scientific Data 9, 764 (2022).
  doi:10.1038/s41597-022-01877-3
- `scripts/download_metropt.py` downloads the 218 MB archive and checks its SHA-256
  (`aab991a9…21a`, verified 2026-09-14). Raw data is never committed.

### What was checked in the file, not assumed

| Claim | Where it comes from | What the file shows |
|---|---|---|
| "logged at 1Hz" | UCI description and archive PDF | **false for this CSV** |
| "collected at 0.1Hz" | UCI attribute information | median interval 10 s; 99.97 % of intervals between 9 and 13 s |
| 1,516,948 instances | UCI page | 1,516,948 rows |
| continuous | none | 331 gaps longer than 60 s, about 910 h in total |

### Ground truth: four failure reports

Transcribed from `Data Description_Metro.pdf` inside the archive.

| # | Start | End | Failure | Reported maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2020-04-18 00:00 | 2020-04-18 23:59 | air leak | none given |
| 2 | 2020-05-29 23:30 | 2020-05-30 06:00 | air leak | "30Apr at 12:00" (read as **30 May**, see below) |
| 3 | 2020-06-05 10:00 | 2020-06-07 14:30 | air leak | 2020-06-08 16:00 |
| 4 | 2020-07-15 14:30 | 2020-07-15 19:00 | air leak | 2020-07-16 00:00 |

Ambiguities, written down because they change the evaluation:

1. The PDF numbers the reports #1, #1, #3, #4; they are renumbered 1-4.
2. Report 2 gives a maintenance date one month **before** the failure. It is
   interpreted as 30 May 12:00. This only affects which alerts are excused as
   post-failure aftermath.
3. Four events is a very small sample. Every detection count on this track is
   reported next to a chance level (circular-shift control) for that reason.

### Preprocessing

Samples are aligned to a 10 s grid (`align_to_grid`), empty slots stay NaN,
the cleaner holds a missing value for at most 30 s, and a 30-minute window with
more than 20 % missing samples produces no decision at all. The split is
chronological and was fixed before any detector was evaluated:

| Period | Dates | Use |
|---|---|---|
| train | 2020-02-01 to 2020-03-15 | fit |
| calibration | 2020-03-15 to 2020-04-01 | threshold and persistence |
| test | 2020-04-01 to 2020-09-01 | all four failure reports |

Nothing guarantees that February and March were healthy: they are only free of
failure **reports**.
